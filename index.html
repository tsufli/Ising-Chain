Each arrow is the local spin expectation (⟨σˣ⟩, ⟨σᶻ⟩): it points up in the ordered phase and tilts toward the field direction as h grows.

What am I looking at?

Method. The Hamiltonian acts on 2N basis states but is never stored as a matrix: its action on a vector is applied directly with bit operations. The lowest eigenstates come from the Lanczos algorithm. H commutes with the global spin flip Πσˣ, so the even and odd parity sectors are solved separately: the ground state is the lowest even state, and the lowest odd state gives the gap.

Phase transition. For h < J the chain is ferromagnetic and the two lowest states become degenerate (the gap closes exponentially in N). For h > J the field wins and the spins align along x. The critical point is at h = J; on a finite chain the transition is rounded, and it sharpens as you increase N.

Why symmetry breaking is simulated. In a finite chain the exact ground state is symmetric under the spin flip, so ⟨σᶻᵢ⟩ is exactly 0. The arrows therefore show the state (ψeven + ψodd)/√2, which is the symmetry-broken ground state when the gap is tiny. The order parameter plotted in the charts, m² = ⟨(Σσᶻ)²⟩/N², is well defined for the exact ground state. A small residual arrow length in the disordered phase is a finite-size effect that shrinks as N grows.

Spectrum. The lowest levels of the even (solid) and odd (dashed) parity sectors, measured from the ground state, as a function of h. In the ordered phase the lowest odd level merges with the ground state (the two symmetry-broken states become degenerate); at h = J the gap is smallest relative to its thermodynamic-limit value and then reopens linearly in the paramagnet. Degenerate levels, such as the ±k momentum pairs on a ring, are drawn once.

Correlations. C(r) = ⟨σᶻᵢσᶻᵢ₊ᵣ⟩ in the exact ground state, averaged over i, for the current h, N and boundary. It stays close to m² at large r in the ordered phase (long-range order) and decays quickly in the disordered phase. Since ⟨σᶻ⟩ = 0 in the symmetric ground state, this is also the connected correlator.

Curves. The spectrum and order-parameter curves are computed for the current N and boundary condition (the sweep fills in progressively, coarse first); the dots and vertical line mark your slider position.
